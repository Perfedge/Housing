Households and homes

ACS 2020–2024: 4,651 households. Stock of 5,138 is carried forward from earlier work and has not been reverified. Both are proxies for 2026. Start-of-2026 to start-of-2040 is 14 years.

Unavailable stock includes second homes and units that cannot house local households. A zero default is optimistic, not evidence that all vacant homes are available. Annual completions are net additions after losses, starting after the baseline.

Local workforce policy

Historical EPS workbook inputs, not 2026 measurements. This alternative asks how many homes would support a chosen share of local workers living here. It omits vacancy allowances and households without workers. Do not add it to the household-growth estimate.

Income and rental / ownership mix

Historical EPS working-age income shares are a proxy, applied here to all projected need. They are not current tenure-by-income data. Published income limits changed since that classification. Each rental share defaults to the ACS overall 39.9%, an assumption for each band.

Building types

Choose a mix for each tenure. Each column must total 100%. These mixes are student choices, not observed preferences or a prediction. Income does not determine building type.

What could a household afford?

Financing inputs are illustrative, not mortgage offers. Ownership includes principal, interest, taxes, insurance, maintenance and HOA. Closing costs, mortgage insurance and credit requirements are excluded. All budgets are in constant 2026 dollars.

Existing affordability needs

35% is an illustrative cost-burden share, not a verified local count. Cost burden means paying over 30% of income for housing. These households already occupy homes; do not add them to the physical unit gap.

What this estimator cannot tell us

This is not a parcel capacity, development feasibility, homelessness, displacement or overcrowding model. It does not measure affordable homes currently available by income and tenure, so it cannot establish a verified affordable-unit shortage. New household formation, migration, aging, household size changes and replacement needs require more evidence. The scenario uses a fixed income distribution, tenure pattern and building mix through 2040.

Before using results for public policy, verify available 2026 stock, net completions and losses, seasonal vacancies, ACS margins of error, current tenure-by-income counts, and the needs of unhoused and doubled-up households. The old EPS sample excludes householders 65 and older and is not representative of all current residents.
